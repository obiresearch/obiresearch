Research Overview

To meet global sustainable development goals, we must balance energy generation and storage with emissions management. The Obi research group explores these challenges through the lens of fundamental main group inorganic chemistry, with a focus on carbon capture and hydrogen storage.

Molecular Calcium Looping

Calcium looping is a relatively novel technology that utilizes calcium oxide raw materials to reversibly capture and release CO2. While this process is reasonably understood at the “bulk” material scale, reversible CO2 capture at molecular calcium metal centers is entirely unknown. Using rational ligand design, we investigate how discrete calcium metal centers can react reversibly with CO2.

Phosphorus-based CO2 capture materials

Phosphines are desirable for CO2 capture due to their high electronic tunability. However, they're significantly limited by their propensity to irreversibly form phosphine oxides in moist/aqueous environments. Here, we explore phosphorus ylides that have been (electro)chemically reduced to simultaneously enhance nucleophilicity for ambient CO2 capture, while reducing Wittig-type phosphine oxide formation pathways.

  • phosphines
  • frustrated Lewis pairs
  • CO2

Mixed metal hydrides

Mixed metal hydrides are promising for rechargeable hydrogen fuels, ensuring emissions-free energy storage under commercial conditions. We explore molecular insights into their structure and reactivity, to enhance their hydrogen storage and release kinetic for broader applications.

  • hydrogen storage
  • metal hydrides
